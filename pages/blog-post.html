Five star Nairobi Experiences
That Don't Require a Safari

James Kariuki · 12 May 2026 · 6 min read
  1. Home
  2. Blog
  3. Five star Nairobi Experiences

Most visitors land at JKIA, spend a night at a 'LUXURY' hotel near the airport, and board a morning flight to Maasai Mara. They leave thinking they have seen Nairobi. Well, theyhaven't. Here is what they miss.

1. Karura Forest at Dawn

Karura, a vast land full of indigenous flora, sits inside the city boundary. At 6:30 in the morning, before the joggers arrive in numbers, the forest is almost silent except for the odd waterfall. Entrance costs KES 600 for non-residents. Bring running shoes or a bicycle — the trails are maintained and well-marked.

We keep a small picnic kit at the hotel that guests can borrow. Ask at reception the evening before and we will have it ready by 6:00 AM.

2. The Saturday Morning Jazz Session at Cultivate

Cultivate is a coffee roastery and community space in Kilimani that hosts a live jazz session every Saturday from 9:00 AM. The musicians are mostly students from the Kenya Conservatoire of Music. The coffee is roasted on-site and genuinely excellent. It fills up by 10:00 so arrive early.

3. Nairobi National Museum

The museum on Museum Hill is one of the better natural history collections in East Africa and most guests walk past it on their way to somewhere else. The ethnography wing on the upper floor — covering Kenya's 42 ethnic groups — is worth two hours of quiet attention. Entry is KES 1,200 for non-residents.

"Nairobi rewards the cunning. The city reveal its self to those who are willing to explore beyond the obvious." — James Kariuki, Director of Guest Experience

4. The Maasai Market (Fridays at Village Market)

The Maasai Market rotates around several Nairobi venues throughout the week. On Fridays it sets up at Village Market in Gigiri. You will find beadwork, textiles, necklaces, soapstone, and carvings, most genuinely handmade. Bargaining is expected. Start at roughly 40% of the first asking price and meet somewhere in the middle.

A word of advice: go between 10:00 AM and noon. The afternoon crowds make it harder to have the quiet conversations with vendors.

5. Dinner in the Karen Suburbs

Karen is a home to a cluster of restaurants that would hold their own in any European city. Our current recommendation is Talisman on Ngong Road: candlelit garden seating, a menu that draws from across the continent, and a wine list assembled by a man of taste.

We can arrange a hotel car and driver for the evening. Speak to the staff — they will book the table and handle the logistics.
